Season's Givings privacy policy

Last updated: November 4, 2026

Season's Givings is a gift planner for iPhone, iPad and Mac, made by Joe Andriano ("the developer"). This page says what happens to what you put in it.

The short version: the app doesn't send the developer anything you put in it. There's no account to make, no server of ours, and no ads, analytics or tracking. What you enter stays on your devices and in your own iCloud, and goes to the people you choose to share it with. The developer receives something about you in only three ways, all described below: a problem report you choose to send, what Apple provides about beta testers, and the anonymous figures Apple may provide if you've agreed to share them.

Where your information is

  • On your device. People, gifts, budgets, notes, wish lists, photos and receipts are kept in the app's storage on your device. The app also keeps a few automatic safety copies there, so it can recover from a problem. "Keeping and deleting" says how long.
  • In your iCloud, if you turn it on. With iCloud sync, your information is stored in your own iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit. It's held by Apple under your Apple Account and Apple's privacy policy. The developer can't see it.
  • With the people you invite. A shared planner, group or wish list is stored in the iCloud of whoever started it, and the people in it can see what's in it. Invitations go by link, and whoever opens a link first joins through it, so send them only to the person you mean. Apple may show each person's name from their Apple Account to the others in a share. Gifts you mark Private, and your private notes and budgets for your partner, stay in your own iCloud and aren't shared.

What leaves your device, and when

Only these, and only because of something you do or turn on:

  • Store pages. When you share or paste a link, the app fetches that page directly from the store's website to read the name, price and photo, as a browser would, and may also use Apple's link previews. The store sees an ordinary request from your device, including its internet address. Nothing goes through the developer.
  • Report a problem. This makes a report you can read first. Send report opens an email to the developer with the report in it, and nothing goes until you send that email. You can send it somewhere else instead. It holds the app and system version, your device model, whether Pro is on, how sync is doing, recent sync error codes, and how many of each kind of record you have. It holds no gifts, people, notes or names. If you email it, the developer also receives your email address and name as your email shows them, and anything you add. The developer keeps these emails only to deal with the problem.
  • Save a copy and Export. These make files with your planner in them, private gifts included if you choose (a copy always includes them). They go where you put them. Keep them somewhere only you can open.
  • Purchases. Season's Givings Pro and tips are bought through Apple. Apple handles the payment, and the developer never sees your card or your Apple Account details. The app checks with Apple on your device whether you have Pro.

What stays on your device

  • Receipts. Scanning or reading a receipt happens on your device, using Apple's text recognition. The app asks for the camera only to scan.
  • Photos. You pick each photo. The app has no access to the rest of your library.
  • Reminders and badges. Reminders for returns and deliveries are set on your device. iCloud may send a silent signal through Apple when something has changed, with nothing of yours in it that the developer can read.
  • Face ID or Touch ID, if you turn on the lock. Apple handles this. The app only learns whether it worked.
  • Siri and widgets. If you use them, what they show comes from your device, and Apple's terms cover Siri.

Crash reports and the beta

The app has no crash reporting of its own. If you've agreed in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give the developer anonymous crash and usage figures.

While the app is in TestFlight, Apple also gives the developer the email address and name you were invited under, your device model and system version, when you installed the beta and how often you've opened it, crash reports, and any feedback you send through TestFlight. It's used only to improve the app. Apple's TestFlight & Privacy page has the details.

During the test, the app is sent out through Anthony Giuliano's Apple developer account. In TestFlight it's called XMas Gift List Beta, from Anthony Giuliano, and on your home screen it's Givings. Because it's his account, he can see what Apple provides about testers, described above. He can't see your gifts or lists. Joe Andriano makes the app and answers for this policy.

Children

A kid's wish list can be set up by a parent, and it can be used on a kid's own device with their own Apple Account. The app sends the developer nothing about a child. A kid's list and ideas are stored in iCloud like everything else, and are seen by the parents who run the list and the people in their group. Apple doesn't allow children under 13 to use TestFlight, so the beta isn't for them to install. If a child emails a problem report, write to the address below and it will be deleted.

Keeping and deleting

You decide, with these limits. Deleting something in the app removes it from your planner on your devices and from iCloud, but not at once from the following:

  • Safety copies on your device. These hold your whole planner as it was when each was made. The app keeps its three newest daily copies, so those are replaced within days of use. It also keeps a few made just before something risky, such as a merge, a restore, or iCloud removing data. Those have no time limit and are replaced only when that happens again, so one can last as long as you keep the app. They stay on that device, and they aren't included in iCloud Backup.
  • Your device's own backups, which Apple holds under its terms, and any copy or export you saved.
  • Other people's devices. Anything you shared may remain with the people you shared it with, in their planner or their safety copies, since it's theirs to keep or delete.

Deleting the app removes what's on that device, including its safety copies. To delete what's in your iCloud, go to Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then Manage Storage, and delete the data for Givings, the name on your home screen. That removes it from iCloud and from all your devices, but each device first makes a safety copy, as above, unless it's out of room. The developer holds none of your planner, so it has none to delete. To have a problem report you emailed deleted, or for what Apple provides about beta testers, write to the address below.
